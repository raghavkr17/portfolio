Mitcham

A marketplace that lets restaurants sell their leftover food cheap before it gets tossed.

Role
Designer + the only engineer
When
2026 · started as Biryani Box
Status
Private beta, adding vendors
Built with
JS · Node/Express · Postgres

The problem

Good food gets thrown out every night just because the day's over.

Bakeries, sweet shops, restaurants, and grocers all end up with food they can't sell. Nothing's wrong with it. They're just closing.

And people nearby would happily pay less for it. There just wasn't an easy way to connect the two.

The idea

Vendors post their extra food as discounted rescue bags. Customers see what's nearby, reserve one, and pick it up with a code before closing.

Any vendor can apply. Once they're approved, they get their own storefront, live bag counts, and a dashboard for reservations. No setup on their end.

Try it live See the code

What I built

  • Three sides to one app. Customers browse and reserve. Vendors manage bags and pickups. Admins approve new vendors.
  • Real accounts with Google sign in. Everyone only sees what their role should see.
  • A full backend I wrote myself. Node/Express API, Postgres on Supabase, migrations, and admin tools.
  • A demo mode. If the backend is down, the app switches to a mock version so anyone can still click around.

Choices I made

  • Pickup codes before payments. Reserve, show a code, grab your food. I kept it short because you're probably already walking to the shop.
  • Zero setup for vendors. Small shops don't have IT people. Applying and getting approved is the whole process.
  • Built for India. Prices are in rupees because that's the market it started in.

Where it came from

Mitcham started as Biryani Box, an ordering system for one restaurant (V1, V2). Then I realized the same idea could work for a ton of vendors at once.

Where it's at

It's in private beta right now. I'm onboarding vendors who are interested before a public launch.
