Mitcham

A two-sided marketplace that connects vendors' end-of-day food surplus with nearby customers, at a discount, before it goes to waste.

Role
Designer & sole full-stack engineer
Timeline
2026 · grew from Biryani Box
Status
Private beta, onboarding vendors
Stack
Vanilla JS · Node/Express · PostgreSQL

The problem

Good food gets thrown away every night, simply because the day is over.

Restaurants, bakeries, sweet shops, and grocers end up with food they can't sell. It isn't bad, the shop is just closing. Meanwhile, nearby customers would happily pay less for it. Nothing connects the two in time.

The idea

Vendors list surplus as discounted rescue bags. Customers browse nearby vendors, reserve a bag, and pick it up before closing using a simple code. Less waste, cheaper food, and extra revenue on food that would have been binned.

Any vendor can apply, get approved, and instantly have a storefront, live bag counts, and a reservation dashboard, with nothing to set up on their side.

What I built

  • Three experiences in one product: customer (Nearby Bags, Reservations), vendor (storefront, live bag counts, reservation dashboard), and admin (approval and oversight).
  • Real accounts via Sign in with Google, with role-based access so each person only sees what they should.
  • A full backend: a REST API in Node/Express, a PostgreSQL database on Supabase, database migrations, and admin tooling.
  • A demo mode: if no backend is configured, the app falls back to a self-contained in-browser mock with a persona picker, so anyone can explore the UI without signing in.

Design decisions

Pickup codes over payments-first. The core promise is simple: reserve, show a code, collect. Keeping that flow short matters more than a long checkout when someone is walking to a shop before it closes.

Vendor onboarding with no setup. Small shops don't have an IT team. Applying and being approved should be the entire process.

Prices in rupees, built for India. The product is designed around the local food-surplus market it started from.

Where it started

Mitcham began as Biryani Box, a single-restaurant ordering system (V1, V2), then grew into a general platform for rescuing surplus across many vendors at once.

Status & next

Mitcham is in private beta, and I'm starting to onboard interested vendors ahead of a public launch.
